About JordanWatch

JordanWatch rates lakes Avoid, Caution or Low Risk for harmful algal blooms, so swimmers, paddlers and dog owners can check before they go in the water. It was built for Jordan Lake in North Carolina and works for about 7,000 lakes across the US that EPA's satellites cover. It's free, needs no account, and has no ads.

How it works

Satellite. EPA's CyAN project estimates cyanobacteria (the algae behind harmful blooms) from satellite images, in cells per milliliter, with pixels about 300 m wide. JordanWatch compares them with the World Health Organization's 1999/2003 guideline levels: under 20,000 cells/mL is Low Risk, 20,000 to 100,000 is Caution, and over 100,000 is Avoid.

Cells, not toxins. NC DHHS and EPA judge recreation by a toxin, microcystin (8 µg/L), which needs a water test. Satellites can't measure toxins, so JordanWatch uses WHO's cell-count levels as an early warning.

Other signals. Heavy rain in the last 48 hours or a river running higher than usual can wash bacteria into the water, so they can raise a verdict to Caution. So can a lake well above its usual level. Light wind can pile floating algae on the downwind shore, so beaches there go up one level.

The worst signal wins. Each verdict is the worst of its signals, and the Why card shows every one of them. Old or missing data never gives an all-clear: a reading over a week old can keep a warning but can't give Low Risk.

Camera scan. A model trained on photos of blooms, plants, clear water and shorelines looks at what your camera sees. It's a screening aid, not a diagnosis: it says Uncertain when it isn't sure, and a bloom you confirm near one of Jordan Lake's areas raises that area's verdict, on your phone only.

Every verdict is worked out on your phone. Your exact location stays on it.

Not an official advisory

JordanWatch gives estimates from public data, not lab tests. Satellites can't measure toxins, can't see narrow arms or the shoreline, and can be days old. Always follow posted signs and your state's advisories, and stay out of water that looks green, scummy or like spilled paint.

If you, a child or a pet went in the water

People

Rinse off with tap water right away. If you feel sick, contact a healthcare provider or Poison Control.

Poison Control 1-800-222-1222

Pets

Rinse them with tap water right away, before they lick their fur. If they seem sick, call a veterinarian immediately. Animal poison lines charge a fee.

ASPCA Animal Poison Control 1-888-426-4435 Pet Poison Helpline 1-855-764-7661
